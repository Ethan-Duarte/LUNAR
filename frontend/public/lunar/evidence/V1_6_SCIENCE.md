# V1.6 scientific evidence

See HORIZON_WHEEL_AUDIT.md for pinned primary-source links and scope. The original peer tests passed 49/49; matched adapter tests passed 2/2. These scalar and synthetic-horizon comparisons do not certify measured-terrain or end-to-end mission equivalence.

LUNAR's 30-day Connecting Ridge electrical case uses actual shipped JPL/LOLA inputs and explicitly relaxed slope/Earth requirements. Demand is 720 kWh. Initial storage is 50 kWh; at 95% discharge efficiency it can deliver only 47.5 kWh. With zero solar, unmet energy is therefore 672.5 kWh, reproduced by the worker. At 20 m², generated energy is 4028.2154 kWh and unmet energy zero; at 40 m² it is 8056.4308 kWh and unmet energy zero. Both end at 250 kWh stored; extra generation does not automatically improve the design. See raw curtailment and losses in qa/v16/month_power.json. This is one finite electrical case, not demonstrated sustainability or actual infrastructure siting.

New timing masks combine supplied demand, end-of-step reserve, electrical connectivity and Earth contact if required. Delivered sunlight is shown independently, so night operation on battery is allowed. Static terrain/separation constraints are excluded from that timing mask and remain authoritative in the full verdict. Neither a probability nor a DSN-contact certificate is produced.

An independent event check uses NASA GSFC's published 2025-03-14 lunar eclipse prediction: https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/LEplot/LEplot2001/LE2025Mar14T.pdf. Greatest eclipse is listed as 06:58:41.7 UT. With common hourly JPL input and a one-minute scan, LUNAR's Moon-centre Sun/Earth alignment minimum is at 06:59 UTC (18.3 seconds difference; one-minute grid). A five-minute tolerance was declared in the test. At a controlled equatorial zero-elevation observer, the modeled Sun is fully occulted at 06:45 and 07:15 and clear at 03:00 and 11:00. This checks coarse event timing and interior/exterior classification. It does not compare local contact times to global lunar-eclipse contacts; those are different observables. Atmosphere, exact terrain/eclipse mask integration and all-site contact timing remain unvalidated.

The fixed Earth-map holdout still fails, as reproduced in V1.5. No input pixels, locations or thresholds changed. Python scientific production code is unchanged here. Feature parity remains incomplete, particularly DSN access, terrain-clone uncertainty and traverse planning.
